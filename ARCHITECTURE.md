# Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B — what the config forces on the design

Source: [`Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B/config.json`](https://hf.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B/blob/main/config.json)
(published 8 Aug 2026). Numbers below are derived, not estimated.

```
hidden_size            8192      num_hidden_layers        92
num_experts            512       num_experts_per_tok      10
moe_intermediate_size  2048      shared_expert_inter_size 2048
vocab_size           248320      head_dim                256
num_attention_heads      64      num_key_value_heads       4
layer_types    [linear ×3, full] ×23   ->  69 linear + 23 full attention
mtp_num_hidden_layers     1      max_position_embeddings 262144
```

## Parameter decomposition

One expert is a SwiGLU triple (gate, up, down) at 8192x2048:

```
params/expert = 3 x 8192 x 2048 = 50.33 M
routed total  = 92 layers x 512 experts x 50.33M = 2.371 T   <- the "2.4T"
active routed = 92 layers x  10 experts x 50.33M =  46.3 B
```

Everything else runs on **every** token:

| Component | Params |
| --- | --- |
| Linear-attention layers (69) | ~30.1 B |
| Full-attention layers (23) | ~9.6 B |
| Shared expert (92 x 50.33M) | 4.6 B |
| Embeddings + lm_head (untied) | 4.1 B |
| **Always-hot subtotal** | **~48.4 B** |
| Active routed experts | 46.3 B |
| **Total active/token** | **~94.7 B** ✓ matches A95B |

## The finding that reframes everything

**Slightly over half the active parameters are not experts.** 48.69 B of the
95 B is attention + shared expert + router, touched on every single token
(embeddings are a gather, not a matmul, and cost ~0 bandwidth). That half can
never be evicted, so it is a fixed RAM tax paid before expert caching begins.
Using *real* bits-per-weight — k-quants carry block scales, so Q4_K_M is 4.53
bpw, Q2_K is 2.63, not the nominal 4 and 2:

| Attention quant | Always-hot RAM | Left for expert cache (of 27.5 GB) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q8_0 (8.50 bpw) | 48.2 GB | **negative** — does not fit |
| Q4_K_M (4.53) | 25.7 GB | 1.8 GB |
| Q3_K_M (3.44) | 19.5 GB | 8.0 GB |
| Q2_K (2.63) | 14.9 GB | 12.6 GB |

At Q4_K_M attention, 1.8 GB of cache against 11.1 GB of per-token expert demand
(IQ2_XXS) is 16% residency, and 9.3 GB must come from disk every token — ~0.6
tok/s, *no matter how good the cache policy is*. The hot set is the binding
constraint, not the expert cache. Only at Q2_K attention does the cache get
large enough for policy to matter at all.

The original plan budgeted 12 GB for the hot tier and imagined 16 GB holding
"32 experts". Both were off: the hot tier is 15–26 GB depending on precision,
and one expert is 50.3 M params — **16.5 MB at IQ2_XXS, 28.5 MB at Q4_K_M** —
so 16 GB holds 560–970 experts. The model contains 47,104 experts total.

--model qwen3.8-2.4t-a95b --hit 0.75` (hot set 48.69 B params, pool 2370.8 B,

**Qwen3.8-2.4T does not reach the 2 tok/s aim on a 32 GB box at any precision.**
It scrapes the 1 tok/s floor only at Q2_K attention + IQ1_S experts — quantizing
attention to ~2.6 bits and experts to ~1.6 bits, which is quality-destructive.
Anything preserving attention at 4-bit or better tops out at 0.67 tok/s.

Three structural reasons, none fixable by better I/O engineering:

1. The 46.75 B always-hot half costs 46.3 GB at Q8_0 — **more than the whole
   32 GB machine**. It must be quantized below 4-bit just to leave *any* room.
2. Per-token expert demand (14.18 GB at Q2_K) is comparable to the entire RAM
   budget, so the cache is capacity-bound, not policy-bound. Cache cleverness
   has almost nothing to work with.
3. In every configuration that clears 0.7 tok/s the bottleneck flips to
   **kernel** — the SIMD dequant rate, not DRAM or disk. That constant
   (`kernel_gbps: 24`) is an unverified estimate and must be measured on the
   real CPU before these rows mean anything.

Note also that 900 GB of free space rules out Q8_0 experts (2394 GB) and
Q4_K_M experts (1298 GB) outright, independent of speed.

## The multiplier the plan under-weighted

`mtp_num_hidden_layers: 1` — **the model ships its own Multi-Token Prediction
head.** No separate 1B/3B draft model is needed; the draft is native and shares
the trunk.

This matters more than the cache, because on a bandwidth-bound system verifying
k tokens in one weight sweep amortizes the sweep. It is not a clean k× — the
union of experts over k tokens grows (10 of 512 per token, so 3 tokens touch
~26 distinct experts, not 10) — but the 12.1 GB hot set is amortized fully:

```
1 token  : 12.1 + 11.6            = 23.7 GB  ->  23.7 GB/token
3 tokens : 12.1 + (2.6 x 11.6)    = 42.3 GB  ->  14.1 GB/token   = 1.7x
```

Applied to the Q2_K/IQ2_XXS row (0.92 tok/s single-stream), MTP speculation at
k=3 / 60% acceptance gives **1.30 tok/s** per `capacity.py` — enough to clear
the floor while keeping experts at ~2 bits rather than ~1.6. That is the most
valuable single lever on the server, and it is free: the head ships with the
model. (An earlier draft said 1.5–1.6; that used a cleaner k× amortization than
the model's expert-union growth actually allows.)

## Verdict on the two machines

- **32 GB server, Qwen3.8-2.4T: the 2 tok/s aim is out of reach.** The 1 tok/s
  floor is reachable only via MTP speculation plus ~2-bit attention. The blocker
  is that the always-hot 48.69 B half costs more than the machine has, so RAM is
  consumed before expert caching begins. **More RAM does not fix it**: sweeping
  `ram_total_gb` in `capacity.py` gives 32 GB → 1.30, 48 GB → 1.43, and
  64/96/128 GB → 1.43, flat. Past ~48 GB the model is dequant-compute-bound.
  Reaching 2 tok/s needs a faster kernel (AVX-512, or a GPU for the hot half),
  not more DDR4 and not faster storage.
- **This laptop: Qwen3.8-2.4T cannot run at any precision.** 445 GB at the most
  extreme quantization against 80 GB free; the BF16 repo is 213 shards, ~4.5 TB.
  A storage wall, not a speed problem.

## What the laptop *can* run

`python -m minillm.capacity frontier --machine laptop-i5-8350u --target 1.0
--expert-bits mxfp4` gives the admissible envelope, assuming a perfect cache:

| Active-routed | Active GB | Cacheable | Miss GB | Ceiling tok/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.00 B | 0.99 | 100% | 0.00 | 2.41 |
| **3.58 B** | **1.77** | **100%** | **0.00** | **1.83** |
| 5.00 B | 2.47 | 100% | 0.00 | 1.51 |
| 8.00 B | 3.96 | 63% | 1.46 | 0.35 |
| 17.00 B | 8.41 | 30% | 5.91 | 0.09 |

The cliff between 5 B and 8 B active is where per-token expert demand outgrows
the RAM cache. Combined with the disk ceiling (80 GB at 4.25 bpw = 162 B total
params), a model must satisfy **both**: total ≤ ~162 B *and* active-routed ≤ ~5 B.

**`openai/gpt-oss-120b` sits inside both bounds** — 116.8 B total, 3.58 B
active-routed — and is the largest model found that does. Its 4-of-128 routing
over narrow 2880-wide experts is what makes it fit where a 235B-A22B or
DeepSeek-class model cannot.
